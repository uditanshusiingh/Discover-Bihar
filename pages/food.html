BEYOND THE PLATE

Stories Behind the Flavours

Bihar's food reflects its geography, festivals, agricultural traditions and everyday life.

01 THE BIHAR CLASSIC

Litti Chokha

Simple ingredients. Bold character. A dish that travelled far beyond Bihar.

Litti is traditionally made from wheat flour and filled with sattu, herbs and spices. It is roasted before being served with chokha, a preparation of mashed vegetables.

What began as an everyday food became one of Bihar's most recognisable culinary symbols.

Main Ingredient Sattu
Style Roasted
Served With Chokha
02 SILAO · NALANDA

Khaja

Layers of crispness wrapped in generations of tradition.

Khaja is particularly associated with Silao in Nalanda district and is known for its distinctive layered texture.

It is often enjoyed as a traditional sweet while travelling through the historic Nalanda and Rajgir region.

Origin Silao
Texture Crisp & Layered
Type Sweet
03 CHHATH TRADITION

Thekua

A humble sweet with deep ritual significance.

Thekua is prepared with wheat flour, jaggery and ghee and is traditionally shaped before being fried or baked.

During Chhath Puja, Thekua is closely associated with offerings and family traditions across Bihar.

Festival Chhath Puja
Sweetener Jaggery
Tradition Homemade
04 FESTIVE FLAVOUR

Malpua

Sweet, soft and festive — a familiar flavour across generations.

Malpua is prepared as a sweet pancake and commonly served with sugar syrup. Variations of the dish are enjoyed throughout eastern India.

In Bihar, it is often associated with celebrations, festivals and traditional home cooking.

Type Sweet Pancake
Served With Sugar Syrup
Occasion Festivals
