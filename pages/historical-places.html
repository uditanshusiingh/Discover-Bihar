BEYOND THE LANDMARK

Stories Written in Stone

Bihar's historic places are more than monuments. They are living chapters of India's intellectual, spiritual and architectural heritage.

01 BODH GAYA · GAYA

Mahabodhi Temple

A place where the search for enlightenment became one of the defining spiritual stories of humanity.

Bodh Gaya is traditionally regarded as the place where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment beneath the Bodhi tree. The Mahabodhi Temple complex grew around this sacred memory and became an important pilgrimage destination for Buddhists from across the world.

The temple's soaring brick structure, sacred Bodhi tree and surrounding monuments create an atmosphere where architecture and spirituality come together.

Location Bodh Gaya
Known For Buddhist Heritage
UNESCO World Heritage Site
Discover Bodh Gaya
02 NALANDA · BIHAR

Nalanda Mahavihara

One of the ancient world's great centres of learning, where knowledge travelled across borders.

Nalanda Mahavihara developed into a major Buddhist monastic and intellectual centre in ancient India. Scholars and students travelled from different parts of Asia to study philosophy, logic, medicine, astronomy and other fields of knowledge.

Today, its extensive brick ruins offer a glimpse into the scale and organisation of an ancient residential university complex.

Location Nalanda
Known For Ancient Learning
UNESCO World Heritage Site
Explore Nalanda
03 RAJGIR · NALANDA

Rajgir

Ancient Magadha, sacred landscapes and stories of Buddha and Mahavira meet among the hills of Rajgir.

Rajgir was an important centre of the ancient kingdom of Magadha. Surrounded by hills, the city is associated with important events in the histories of Buddhism and Jainism.

Places such as Vulture Peak and the ancient fortification remains connect Rajgir's natural landscape with its long religious and political history.

Location Rajgir
Known For Magadha & Spiritual Heritage
Landscape Five Sacred Hills
Discover Rajgir
04 PATNA · BIHAR

Golghar

An unmistakable domed landmark that has become part of Patna's architectural identity.

Golghar was constructed in Patna in the late eighteenth century as a granary following a period of severe famine. Its distinctive beehive-like form and massive brick structure make it one of the city's most recognisable historic landmarks.

Standing beside the Ganga, Golghar also offers a striking connection between Patna's colonial-era architecture and its riverfront landscape.

Location Patna
Built 18th Century
Known For Historic Granary
